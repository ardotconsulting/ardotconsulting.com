Most businesses hear "AI" and think of one of two things: either the sci-fi version where robots run everything, or the hype version where every problem gets solved by throwing ChatGPT at it.

Neither is accurate. Let's talk about what's actually happening.

## What AI automation looks like today

AI today is narrow, practical, and — in the best way — boring. It's not replacing your team. It's taking the tasks nobody wants to do and doing them faster, more consistently, and without complaints.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

- **Document processing**: AI reads invoices, contracts, and forms, extracts the relevant data, and puts it where it needs to go. No more manual data entry.
- **Data reporting**: Instead of someone spending three hours every Monday pulling data from five systems into a spreadsheet, AI does it automatically and sends the report on schedule.
- **Customer inquiry triage**: Incoming emails and messages get categorized and routed to the right person — or answered automatically for common questions.
- **Workflow orchestration**: When a new client onboards, AI triggers the right sequence of tasks — welcome email, account setup, document collection — without anyone having to remember the checklist.

## What AI can't do (yet)

Setting honest expectations matters more than selling a dream. Here's what AI is **not** good at right now:

1. **Anything requiring genuine judgment** — AI can summarize a contract, but a lawyer should review the risks. AI can draft a marketing email, but a human should approve the tone.
2. **Tasks with no pattern** — If a task is different every single time and has no repeatable structure, AI won't help much.
3. **Replacing human relationships** — Your clients want to talk to a person. AI can handle the scheduling, but not the conversation.

## How to know if AI is right for you

Before investing in any AI tool, ask yourself three questions:

1. **Is there a task that takes more than 2 hours per week and follows a pattern?** If yes, there's likely an automation opportunity.
2. **Is someone on your team doing work that a computer could do?** If they're copying data, formatting reports, or answering the same questions repeatedly, that's a candidate.
3. **Would freeing up that time let your team do higher-value work?** The ROI of AI isn't about cutting costs — it's about redirecting human effort to things that actually require a human.

## Where to start

Don't try to automate everything at once. Pick one workflow — the most painful, most repetitive one — and start there. Measure the time saved. Then decide what to automate next.

That's it. No magic, no hype. Just identify the boring work, let a system handle it, and let your team focus on what matters.
